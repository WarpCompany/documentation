</Note>

Warp Verify issues attestations: signed, on-chain-recorded statements that a claim about an asset is true, backed by a zero-knowledge proof. A verifier can confirm the claim without seeing the private data behind it.

## Attestation types

| Type | Proves | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ownership` | The holder controls or owns an asset | A company owns a property without revealing the deed |
| `compliance` | An asset or holder meets a policy | An invoice is within an approved limit without revealing the amount |
| `provenance` | An asset's origin or history | A carbon credit comes from a registered project |

Attestations can describe real-world assets such as real estate, invoices and receivables, equity, commodities, carbon credits and intellectual property.

## Lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create">
    In the [Warp Platform](https://platform.usewarp.net), open your project's **Warp Verify** section, choose a type and describe the asset and policy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prove and attest">
    Warp compiles the circuit, generates the proof and records the attestation in an on-chain registry. The attestation becomes `active`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share and check">
    Share the attestation ID or its public check page (`https://platform.usewarp.net/verify/check/<id>`). Anyone can check it with the API below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Renew or revoke">
    Attestations can expire. Renew them before they lapse, or revoke them if the claim no longer holds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check an attestation

[Check an attestation](/api-reference/attestations/check) is public: it needs no API key and is rate limited per IP.

```bash
curl https://api.usewarp.net/v1/attestations/$ATTESTATION_ID
```

```json
{
  "id": "0b6a...",
  "status": "active",
  "type": "ownership",
  "valid": true,
  "expires_at": "2027-10-14T00:00:00.000Z",
  "chain_id": 84532,
  "registry_address": "0x..."
}
```

`valid` is `true` only when a real proof was recorded for the attestation. Treat `valid: false`, or a `status` such as `revoked` or `expired`, as a failed check.
